Conditions

Open the Conditions page

BlindedCondition

Certain creatures and spells can impair a creature's sight, leaving it Blinded. While Blinded:

  • The creature can't see, and it automatically fails any check that requires sight.
  • The creature has Disadvantage on attack rolls that require sight.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage.

BleedingCondition

Some attacks wound a creature deeply enough to make it bleed. While Bleeding:

  • The creature takes 2 Bleeding damage at the start of each of its turns, and 2 Bleeding damage for every 5 feet it moves on its turn.
  • A creature that's Bleeding can spend 2 AP to hold its wound closed, taking no Bleeding damage from movement until the end of its turn.
  • Any healing from a spell ends the condition.
  • Creatures without blood, such as Constructs and Undead, are immune to this condition.
  • Creatures that regenerate each turn, such as Trolls, are immune to this condition.

CharmedCondition

A creature that fails to resist a magical enchantment can become Charmed. While Charmed:

  • The creature can't attack the charmer or target it with harmful abilities or magical effects.
  • The charmer has Advantage on any check to interact socially with the creature.

DazedCondition

A creature hit hard enough, or caught in the right circumstances, can become Dazed. While Dazed:

  • The creature has Disadvantage on Opportunity Attacks.
  • The creature has Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.
  • Whenever the creature fails a Dexterity saving throw, it falls Prone.

DeafenedCondition

Certain creatures and spells can impair a creature's hearing, leaving it Deafened. While Deafened:

  • The creature can't hear, and it automatically fails any check that requires hearing.
  • Any spell that requires the target to hear a word, command or spoken phrase, such as the Command spell or any of the Power Word spells, has no effect on a Deafened creature.

DrenchedCondition

A creature that has been submerged in water, or is soaked thoroughly with a liquid, becomes Drenched. While Drenched:

  • The creature has Disadvantage on saving throws against spells that deal Lightning damage. If the spell uses an attack roll instead, the attacker has Advantage.
  • A creature soaked in water has Resistance to Fire damage. If it takes Fire damage from a spell that targets an area, it still resists that damage, but the water evaporates and the condition ends.
  • If the creature is soaked in a flammable liquid, such as oil or liquor, any Fire or Lightning damage it takes ignites it, ending this condition and making it Enflamed.
  • The condition ends after 1 minute.

EnflamedCondition

A creature that is set on fire, engulfed in flame or set ablaze becomes Enflamed. While Enflamed:

  • The creature takes 1d6 Fire damage at the start of each of its turns.
  • The creature can spend 2 AP on its turn to put out the flames, ending the condition.
  • Becoming Drenched in water ends the condition.
  • Creatures immune to Fire damage are immune to this condition.

FrightenedCondition

A creature whose safety is put at risk can become Frightened. While Frightened:

  • The creature has Disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls while the source of its fear is within its line of sight.
  • The creature can't willingly move closer to the source of its fear.

GrappledCondition

A grapple is an attack that deals no damage but holds a target in place. While Grappled:

  • The creature's speed becomes 0, and it can't benefit from any bonus to its speed.
  • If a creature is Grappled by another creature, it can spend 1 AP on its turn to try to escape, making either a Strength (Athletics) check or a Dexterity (Acrobatics) check contested by the grappler's Strength (Athletics) check. On a success, the condition ends.
  • The condition ends if the grappler becomes Incapacitated.
  • The condition ends if an effect moves the Grappled creature out of the grappler's reach, such as being thrown or pushed away.

IncapacitatedCondition

A creature that is impaired and unable to use certain limbs or act clearly becomes Incapacitated. While Incapacitated:

  • The creature can't spend AP or MP, and it can't take reactions.
  • At the start of its turn, the creature can't roll its Action Die.
  • The creature can't gain Action Points (AP) or Magic Points (MP).

InvisibleCondition

An Invisible creature can't be seen by normal means, without the aid of magic or a special sense. Its location can still be detected by any noise it makes or tracks it leaves. While Invisible:

  • Attack rolls against the creature have Disadvantage.
  • The creature's attack rolls have Advantage.
  • For the purpose of hiding, the creature is heavily obscured.
  • When a creature tries to locate a nearby Invisible creature, the Invisible creature makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check against the searcher's Passive Perception. On a failure, its location is revealed.

MutedCondition

A creature can be Muted by many things, from magical spells to having its tongue cut out. While Muted:

  • The creature can't speak, and it can't communicate through spoken language.
  • The creature can't cast spells that require a Verbal Component.

Note: spell cards in the Spell Compendium now list Components, so Muted blocks any spell marked Verbal.

ParalyzedCondition

Paralysis can be caused by many things, from a creature's toxic venom to a wizard's spell. While Paralyzed:

  • The creature is Incapacitated (see the condition), and it can't move or speak.
  • The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage.
  • Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 feet of it.

Designer's note: effects that cause Paralyzed define their own duration or a saving throw that repeats without an AP cost, since an Incapacitated creature can't spend AP.

PetrifiedCondition

A Petrified creature has been turned to stone or another solid material. While Petrified:

  • The creature, along with any nonmagical object it's wearing or carrying, is transformed into a solid, inanimate substance (usually stone). Its weight increases tenfold.
  • The creature is Incapacitated (see the condition), can't gain Exhaustion Points (EP), can't move or speak, can't age, and is unaware of its surroundings.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage.
  • The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
  • The creature has Resistance to all damage.
  • The creature is immune to poison and disease, although any poison or disease already in its system is suspended, not cured.

PoisonedCondition

A creature that has been exposed to a toxin can become Poisoned. While Poisoned:

  • The creature has Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks.

Designer's note: Poisoned covers toxins, venoms and poisons. Sickened covers disease and nausea.

ProneCondition

A Prone creature has been knocked down and is lying on the ground. While Prone:

  • The creature's only movement option is to crawl, unless it stands up, which ends the condition. Standing up costs half the creature's speed for that turn.
  • The creature has Disadvantage on attack rolls.
  • An attack roll against the creature has Advantage if the attacker is within 5 feet of it. Otherwise, the attack roll has Disadvantage.

RestrainedCondition

A creature can be Restrained by many things, from entangling spells to the webs of giant spiders. While Restrained:

  • The creature's speed becomes 0, and it can't benefit from any bonus to its speed.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage, and the creature's attack rolls have Disadvantage.
  • The creature has Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.

Designer's note: no default escape rule. Each effect that Restrains defines how it ends.

SickenedCondition

A creature afflicted by a disease or overwhelming nausea becomes Sickened. While Sickened:

  • The creature has Disadvantage on Constitution saving throws. Whenever a Sickened creature fails a Constitution saving throw, it loses 1 Action Point (AP) from its Action Pool while convulsing or vomiting.
  • The creature has Disadvantage on all skill checks.

Designer's note: a deliberately nasty debuff aimed at martial characters, who rely on AP. A creature with 0 AP loses nothing.

StunnedCondition

A creature can be Stunned by certain martial and magical abilities. While Stunned:

  • The creature can't use Action Points (AP) or Magic Points (MP), can't move, and can speak only falteringly.
  • The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage.

Designer's note: unlike Incapacitated, a Stunned creature still rolls its Action Die and gains points. It just can't spend them until the stun ends (capped by Max Pool). A stun delays a turn rather than deleting it.

UnconsciousCondition

A creature that is asleep, knocked out or held in a fugue state is Unconscious. While Unconscious:

  • The creature is Incapacitated (see the condition), can't move or speak, and is unaware of its surroundings.
  • When a creature becomes Unconscious, it loses all Action Points (AP) and Magic Points (MP) currently in its Action Pool.
  • The creature drops whatever it's holding and falls Prone.
  • The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
  • Attack rolls against the creature have Advantage.
  • Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 feet of it.

Designer's note: Exhaustion Points stay in the pool through unconsciousness.
